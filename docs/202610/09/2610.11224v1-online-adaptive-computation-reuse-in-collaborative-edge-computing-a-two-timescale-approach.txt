# Online Adaptive Computation Reuse in Collaborative Edge Computing: A Two-Timescale Approach 

Xingqiu He, Chaoqun You, Peng Wang, Xiaoyu Liu, and Tony Q. S. Quek, Fellow, IEEE 

Abstract —Collaborative Edge Computing (CEC) is an efficient computing paradigm that enables neighboring edge servers to share computational resources with each other. Although CEC can enhance resource utilization, it still suffers from duplicate computations because nearby end-users often offload tasks with similar inputs. To improve system efficiency, the computation results of previously executed tasks can be cached and reused by subsequent tasks. However, time-varying task popularity and arrival rates require caching and scheduling decisions to adapt to demand changes, while frequent cache updates incur additional costs. To address this issue, this paper develops a two-timescale computation reuse algorithm for CEC networks. We formulate an optimization problem that jointly considers weighted response time and cache update cost, with result caching decisions determined at the frame level and workload scheduling, cache searching, and computational resource alloca-tion adjusted at the slot level. Using recent workload observations, we construct a frame-level surrogate problem and decompose it into a caching subproblem and a scheduling subproblem. For the caching subproblem, we introduce marginal storage efficiency and incorporate cache update costs into a bisection-based algorithm. Under bounded normalized marginal sensitivity, the algorithm achieves a near-optimal objective value for the single-BS caching subproblem when individual result sizes are small relative to the cache capacity and the relaxed solution is sufficiently accurate. For the scheduling subproblem, we utilize projected gradient descent and backtracking with warm starts across consecutive slots. Numerical results demonstrate consistent performance gains over benchmark schemes across diverse network and workload settings. 

Index Terms —Computation Reuse, Collaborative Edge Com-puting, Collaborative Caching, Scheduling Algorithm 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

In the past decade, the rapid evolution of machine learning and the Internet of Things has driven the development of many computation-intensive applications, such as AR/VR [1] and mobile gaming [2]. To fulfill the stringent Quality of Service (QoS) requirements of these applications, edge computing is regarded as a promising computing paradigm that provides computation-offloading services in proximity to end-users.

Nevertheless, due to limited resources at edge servers, an edge computing system may suffer from severe performance degra-dation when dealing with bursty workloads [3]. To address this issue, Collaborative Edge Computing (CEC) [4] has been proposed to alleviate load imbalance by offloading compu-tation tasks from busy edge servers to idle ones. Extensive studies [5]–[8] have shown that CEC effectively improves the utilization of computational resources and thus enhances system performance. Despite the significant improvement CEC brings to resource utilization, computational resources can still be wasted due to duplicate computations. As shown in [9], clustered end-users are likely to offload similar computation tasks to edge servers due to temporal, spatial, or semantic correlations among input data. For example, end-users in AR/VR may request the ren-dering of the same frame if they have similar poses [10], and intelligent surveillance systems often need to analyze similar images captured within a short period of time [9]. Based on this observation, computation reuse [11], [12] has been proposed to further reduce resource waste. The main idea is that the computation results of previously processed tasks can be cached and reused if subsequent tasks have similar inputs. This mechanism not only avoids duplicate computations, but also reduces response time, as tasks with cached results can be completed immediately without being processed from scratch. Computation reuse in CEC networks enables tasks at one edge server to leverage results cached at other servers, thereby enlarging the set of reusable results and reducing redundant caching [13]–[15]. The effectiveness of such collaboration critically depends on how well caching decisions and workload scheduling match the heterogeneous demands across edge servers. In practical CEC networks, however, task popular-ity and arrival rates are inherently time-varying, rendering caching and scheduling decisions optimized for the current demand profile less effective as the workload evolves. Adapt-ing caching decisions to these changes incurs non-negligible communication and storage I/O overhead, making frequent reconfiguration undesirable. In contrast, workload scheduling must react promptly to short-term traffic fluctuations. This disparity in adaptation timescales motivates an online frame-work that jointly optimizes caching decisions and workload scheduling at different timescales. Despite the aforementioned benefits, implementing com-putation reuse in CEC networks under dynamic demand is particularly challenging for three reasons. First, compared to computation reuse at a single edge server, the mutual influence arXiv:2610.11224v1 [cs.NI] 8 Oct 2026 JOURNAL OF L ATEX CLASS FILES, VOL. 14, NO. 8, AUGUST 2021 2

among caching decisions at different edge servers is hard to quantify. Second, compared to conventional CEC networks, computation reuse changes the distribution of task processing time, thereby making caching and scheduling decisions deeply intertwined and difficult to optimize. Third, caching decisions must balance the benefits of adapting to evolving demands against the associated reconfiguration costs, while making decisions online without knowledge of future workloads. In this paper, we propose a two-timescale computation reuse algorithm for CEC networks. Our objective is to simultane-ously minimize the long-term weighted response time and cache update cost by jointly optimizing result caching, cache searching, workload scheduling, and computational power al-location. The formulated problem is a mixed integer nonlinear programming problem and is proved to be NP-hard even for a time-invariant, single-server, single-application special case. In order to address workload uncertainty, we construct a frame-level surrogate problem using recently observed workloads and apply alternating minimization to decompose it into a caching subproblem and a scheduling subproblem. We incorporate the marginal update cost into the storage efficiency used to optimize each relaxed caching subproblem. The resulting de-cisions are converted to binary candidates, which are accepted only when they reduce the overall surrogate objective. Within each frame, the caching decisions remain fixed, while the scheduling decisions are dynamically adjusted to the task arrival rates. The main contributions are summarized as follows.  

> •

We formulate a two-timescale computation reuse prob-lem that jointly accounts for response time and cache update cost in CEC networks. The model captures the coupling between frame-level result caching decisions and slot-level scheduling through cache-search overhead and queueing delay. We construct a frame-level surrogate problem from recent workload observations and decom-pose it into caching and scheduling subproblems.  

> •

For the caching subproblem, we introduce marginal stor-age efficiency and combine its average over recent work-load samples with the marginal update cost to develop a bisection-based algorithm. Our theoretical analysis shows that, under bounded normalized marginal sensitivity, the algorithm achieves a near-optimal objective value for the single-BS caching subproblem when individual result sizes are small relative to the cache capacity and the relaxed solution is sufficiently accurate. An acceptance rule ensures that binary reconstruction and numerical approximation do not increase the frame-level objective.  

> •

For the scheduling subproblem, we develop a projected gradient descent algorithm, in which variable substitution is employed to transform the feasible region into prob-ability simplices, thereby enabling efficient projection. A backtracking line search is incorporated to accelerate convergence and ensure solution stability, while warm-start initialization leverages the solution from the preced-ing slot to reduce computational overhead. We further establish the convergence of the frame-level objective sequence and characterize the computational complexity of the proposed algorithms at both timescales.  

> •

We conduct extensive simulations under time-varying workloads to evaluate the response time, cache up-date cost, convergence behavior, and runtime overhead. The evaluation compares our algorithm against its static counterpart and representative caching and scheduling baselines, with a look-ahead scheme included as a per-formance reference. The results demonstrate consistent performance gains over existing schemes across a wide range of network and workload settings. The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section II, we review related work. Section III introduces the system model and formulates the corresponding optimization problem. Section IV describes the two-timescale algorithm and its theoretical analysis. The numerical results and conclusions are presented in Section V and Section VI, respectively. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Scheduling and Caching in CEC Networks 

Workload scheduling and resource allocation in CEC net-works have been extensively investigated to balance compu-tational loads across edge servers and improve overall system performance. Early studies considered distributed optimization to jointly account for user experience and edge-server power efficiency [5], while online peer offloading was developed to minimize long-term latency subject to time-average energy constraints [6]. Distributed optimization has also been em-ployed to derive cost-effective computation offloading deci-sions [8]. Beyond these objectives, deadline-aware scheduling schemes have been investigated to accommodate the stringent latency requirements of computation tasks [3], [7]. Complementary to workload scheduling, collaborative con-tent caching improves resource utilization by exploiting stor-age resources distributed across edge servers. Existing studies have optimized content caching decisions by accounting for content popularity [16], [17], spectrum efficiency [18], as well as network topology and user demands [19]. More recent stud-ies have jointly considered content caching, request routing, and load balancing in heterogeneous edge networks [20], while online caching schemes based on contextual bandits and feder-ated learning have been developed to accommodate emerging content and facilitate cache sharing among edge servers [21]. Unlike content caching, which primarily reduces the cost of retrieving existing content, computation reuse eliminates redundant task execution by caching and reusing previously computed results, thereby coupling caching decisions with computation scheduling. 

B. Computation Reuse 

Similarities among task inputs create opportunities to avoid redundant computation by identifying and sharing reusable results. Representative systems include Potluck [11] and Fog-gyCache [12], which exploit computation reuse across applica-tions and devices, respectively. Subsequent studies have inves-tigated cache management and reuse-aware task execution at the edge [9], [22], [23]. ICedge [13] and Reservoir [14] lever-age information-centric networking to enable computation JOURNAL OF L ATEX CLASS FILES, VOL. 14, NO. 8, AUGUST 2021 3

Collectively, these systems establish practical mechanisms for discovering, retrieving, and sharing reusable computation results and for directing requests to appropriate edge servers. Complementing these system-oriented efforts, several stud-ies have investigated the optimization of computation reuse in edge networks. III. S YSTEM MODEL AND PROBLEM FORMULATION 

In this section, we describe the considered CEC system with computation reuse and formulate a two-timescale response time minimization problem. Complexity analysis shows that the formulated problem is NP-hard even for a significantly simplified time-invariant special case. 

A. Computation Reuse Model 

As shown in Fig.1, we consider a CEC network with N

BSs indexed by N = {1, 2, . . . , N }. Each BS n ∈ N is co-located with an edge server with computational capacity 

Ccn (in CPU frequency) and storage capacity Csn (in GB), re-spectively. Suppose there are A different applications indexed by A = {1, 2, . . . , A } running in the CEC network. The end-users running these applications may offload computation tasks to nearby BSs with different input data. Taking an AR/VR application for example, the input data of each task indicate the user’s pose, comprised of 3D position and 3D orientation. The output of the task is the rendered frame to be displayed on the user’s head-mounted displays. The system operates on two timescales. Specifically, the time horizon is divided into frames indexed by r ∈ R =

{1, 2, . . . }, and each frame consists of T slots denoted by 

Tr = {(r − 1) T + 1 , (r − 1) T + 2 , . . . , rT }. At the beginning of each frame, the task popularity is estimated from recent observations, and the computation result caching decisions are made accordingly. These caching decisions remain unchanged within the frame, since updating the cached results usually incurs non-negligible communication and storage costs. In contrast, the workload scheduling policy can be adjusted in ev-ery slot according to the current task arrival rates. We assume the task popularity remains approximately unchanged within a frame, while the task arrival rates remain approximately unchanged within a slot. For notational simplicity, we hereafter omit the corresponding index sets from universal quantifiers. Specifically, ∀a, m, n, k, r, t is understood as ∀a ∈ A , m, n ∈N , k ∈ K a, r ∈ R , t ∈ T r , with analogous interpretations applying when only a subset of these indices appears. According to the results in [14], for a given application, computation tasks with sufficiently similar input data usually have the same outputs, so the results of previous tasks can be reused by subsequent tasks. Specifically, for application 

a ∈ A , suppose we have identified a set of typical inputs 

Ka = {1, 2, . . . , K a} from its historical data. If a new task of application a is offloaded from end-users, we can first find the closest typical input using fast and space-efficient mechanisms, such as Locality Sensitive Hashing (LSH) [28] and Feature Hashing (FH) [29]. After that, we compute the similarity between the two inputs according to the chosen metric (e.g., structural similarity [30] or cosine similarity [31]). If the similarity exceeds a certain threshold and the output of the corresponding typical input has been cached, the computation result can be directly returned without processing the task from scratch. Although computation reuse saves a significant amount of computational resources when the target result is cached, it also incurs extra computational costs due to operations like hashing and similarity calculation. Therefore, in some cases, it may be beneficial to directly process tasks without searching for the cached results, especially when the probability of successfully finding the target result is low. D. Online Implementation and Analysis 

The complete two-timescale algorithm is summarized in Algorithm 3. At the beginning of each frame, we first estimate the task popularity and construct the workload sample set from recent observations. The sample-specific scheduling decisions and caching decisions are then optimized alternately. For each BS, Algorithms 1 and 2 generate a binary candidate using the caching objective and the fixed previous-frame cache state. The candidate is accepted only if it reduces the objective of problem (12). After the frame-level iteration terminates, the resulting caching decision is deployed and fixed within the frame. In each slot, the current task arrival rates are observed and the scheduling problem is solved with a warm start. Next, we analyze the convergence of the proposed al-gorithm. At the frame level, the backtracking line search ensures that each scheduling update does not increase D[r],while the acceptance rule ensures that each caching update either decreases the frame-level objective or leaves the current decision unchanged. Since the objective is lower bounded, the resulting objective sequence converges, and the algorithm reaches a fixed point when no further scheduling or caching update yields an improvement. At the slot level, for each fixed cache-searching mode, the alternating updates of y[t],

λ[t], and f [t], together with PGD and backtracking, generate a non-increasing objective sequence. Across the 30 runs, Prop requires an average of 11.44 s per 30-minute frame, including all 60 slot-level updates, whereas VaRe-C requires 1.23 s per frame with the same slot-level controller. Although Prop incurs higher optimization overhead, its average runtime remains negligible relative to the frame duration. Moreover, the current implementation is in Python, which can be substantially slower than lower-level languages such as C/C++ for computationally intensive workloads [38]. Further runtime reductions can therefore be expected through optimized implementations in practical deployments.

C ONCLUSIONS 

In this paper, we have proposed an online two-timescale computation reuse algorithm that coordinates caching and scheduling to balance response time and cache update cost in CEC networks. Using recent workload observations, the algorithm uses bisection guided by marginal storage efficiency for caching and projected gradient descent for scheduling. Our analysis establishes near-optimality for single-BS caching when result sizes are small relative to cache capacity. Simula-tion results demonstrate that our algorithm adapts to dynamic demand and outperforms benchmark methods under various settings, highlighting the benefits of coordinating computation reuse across timescales while accounting for update costs.
