Grouped-Query Attention

Abstract
Long-context inference in autoregressive language models is limited by the key–value (KV) cache. The cache grows with context
length, and every decoded token streams the full cache through memory. Most dynamic eviction methods score token importance
per query head and choose tokens independently. This fits poorly with grouped-query attention (GQA), where several query heads
share one physical KV buffer: if the heads choose different tokens, the serving engine must keep the union of their selections,
inflating the cache by up to the group ratio 𝑟. A simple arithmetic average does not fix this—it dilutes the specialized retrieval heads
that carry factual recall.
We introduce Page-EntroKV, a formal framework for KV-cache eviction that works at the level GQA serving actually uses.
Heads in each physical group are pooled with weights derived from sink-isolated collision (Rényi-2) entropy—one inner product
per head, computed once at prefill with no calibration—so sink heads cannot be mistaken for retrieval heads. The pooled scores are
mapped onto PagedAttention page frames, and eviction runs at the hardware tuple (𝑙, 𝑔, 𝑃𝑏). We formalize the union overhead ratio
and intra-group disagreement, prove an exact identity linking them for two-head groups, and give two-sided bounds for general
ratios. We prove that group-level pooling preserves the eviction budget exactly, derive a finite-context needle-retention guarantee
that arithmetic-mean pooling provably violates, characterize the temperature limits of the entropy-weighted family, and give exact
per-layer cross-group page accounting.
On a pilot architecture (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, 𝑟=6, 28 layers), head-independent replay inflates the cache by up to 5.1× at a
2% budget—with the theoretical certificate firing in the large majority of measured cells—while Page-EntroKV holds the union
overhead at exactly 1.000 across 1,008 pooled measurements. Across a 1,008-observation head census, sink isolation inverts the
sink-to-context weight ordering from a 2.10× unearned sink advantage to a 0.66× deficit. In pilot-scale needle retrieval, entropy-
weighted pooling retains the needle where arithmetic-mean pooling collapses (100% vs. 0% under the pooled-selection criterion at
a 20% budget); strict all-layer retention is reported as a limitation. Retained cardinality is exact for every page size, preliminary
runs show representative QA and code tasks remain solvable at 20% retention, and the mechanism ablations are certified over all 56
of 56 layer–group pairs with zero skipped layers. Larger-model, long-context, and serving phases are pre-registered and in progress.

1. Introduction
Long-context large language models (LLMs) are now de-
ployed for multi-turn agentic workflows, repository-scale code
synthesis, and multi-document legal discovery [29, 30, 4]. Con-
text windows have grown to match, but long-context use re-
mains fragile: reliability degrades with input position [31]. At
context lengths of 32k to 128k+ tokens, decoding hits a hard-
ware boundary: every decoded token streams the entire key–
value (KV) cache from GPU high-bandwidth memory (HBM)
into on-chip SRAM, so the decode phase is strictly memory-
bound and its cost grows linearly with context length [9, 7]. At
practical batch sizes the KV cache alone can occupy most of
the accelerator’s memory, capping concurrency and decoding
throughput.
Production architectures respond by sharing KV buffers
across query heads.
Grouped-query attention (GQA) [3]—
adopted by Llama-3.1, Qwen-2.5, and Mistral [4, 5, 6]—
partitions 𝐻𝑄logical query heads into 𝐻KV physical KV
groups, shrinking the cache by the ratio 𝑟= 𝐻𝑄/𝐻KV. In serv-
ing runtimes such as vLLM [7], these groups are materialized
as block-partitioned tensors of shape [𝑁blocks, 𝐻KV, 𝐵, 𝐷head]:
the sharing is the memory layout itself, not an implementation
detail.

This physical layout breaks a core assumption behind
prevailing eviction heuristics.
Policies such as H2O [10],
SnapKV [11], and Scissorhands [12] compute token importance
per query head and select top-𝑘sets independently. Sibling
heads in a GQA group share one physical buffer, so the engine
must store the union of their selections—up to 𝑟× the intended
budget. We formalize this penalty as the union overhead ratio
(UOR) and connect it exactly to measurable intra-group dis-
agreement (Proposition 1). Arithmetic mean pooling fails from
the other direction: it dilutes specialized retrieval heads [24],
averaging a needle token attended to with mass 1 −𝜖by one
head down to (1 −𝜖)/𝑟, below eviction thresholds.
Page-EntroKV follows three design principles, each cho-
sen against a specific alternative.
Pooling at the physical-
group level rather than the head level preserves GQA’s mem-
ory sharing and keeps the budget exact; head-level selection
cannot do both, because the engine stores whatever union the
heads produce. Discrete attention entropy—one inner product
per head—separates sharp retrieval heads from diffuse back-
ground heads, so weighting heads by inverse entropy ampli-
fies exactly the signals that mean pooling destroys; evaluating
that entropy on sink-isolated distributions keeps attention-sink
heads from being mistaken for retrieval heads. Projecting im-
portance onto discrete PagedAttention page frames before evic-
tion, rather than selecting tokens first, makes the compression
decision coincide with the hardware allocation tuple (𝑙, 𝑔, 𝑃𝑏)
and eliminates internal fragmentation and cache-unrolling over-
head. Figure 1 shows the resulting architecture.
The contributions of this paper are formal, algorithmic, im-
plementational, and pilot-validated:
• Architectural formulation. We formalize the GQA struc-
tural constraint in dynamic KV-cache eviction through
two metrics—the union overhead ratio and intra-group
disagreement—linked by an exact identity for two-head
groups and sandwiched two-sidedly at every ratio (Sec-
tion 3).
• Algorithmic design. We design entropy-weighted group
pooling around discrete sink-isolated Rényi-2 attention en-
tropy, together with a page-frame mapping that evicts ex-
actly at the (𝑙, 𝑔, 𝑃𝑏) tuple (Section 4).
• Theoretical guarantees. We prove strict budget preserva-
tion (UOR ≡1), a finite-context needle-retention bound
under head specialization with sink isolation, the temper-
ature degeneracies of the pooling family, and page-level
union accounting (Section 5).
• Reference implementation and pilot validation.

2. Related work
2.1. Attention efficiency and architectural evolution
Multi-head attention (MHA) [1] pairs every query head with
private key–value buffers.
Multi-query attention (MQA) [2]
collapses all heads onto a single shared buffer. GQA [3] in-
terpolates between the two, cutting KV memory by the group
ratio 𝑟at negligible quality cost, and is now standard in pro-
duction models [4, 5, 6]. A separate line compresses KV states
into low-rank latent vectors, as in the multi-head latent atten-
tion (MLA) of DeepSeek-V2 [32]. These works fix the static
allocation of KV memory. Page-EntroKV addresses the com-
plementary dynamic problem: deciding at inference time which
allocated entries to keep.
2.2. Inference serving engines
IO-aware kernel design minimizes HBM traffic. FlashAtten-
tion [27] tiles attention computation on-chip; FlashAttention-2
adds flash-decoding, improving parallelism for long sequences
and decode workloads [28].
On the systems side, vLLM’s
PagedAttention [7] partitions the KV cache into fixed-size
blocks managed by a block table and reaches near-zero frag-
mentation at high concurrency. Orca raises utilization further
through iteration-level scheduling and continuous batching [8].
Roofline analyses [9] establish the memory-bound character of
decoding. These engines assume the cache is fully material-
ized; they define the physical layout to which any compression
policy must conform. Page-EntroKV treats the page table as a
first-class citizen and evicts at exactly the granularity the engine
allocates.
2.3. Dynamic KV-cache eviction
H2O [10] retains tokens with the largest accumulated at-
tention scores. StreamingLLM [13] preserves attention sinks
and a sliding window. Scissorhands [12] exploits the persis-
tence of token importance. SnapKV [11] compresses the pre-
fill cache using observation windows.
PyramidKV [15] al-
locates layer-wise budgets following a pyramidal information
funnel.
Quest [16] performs query-aware sparse selection.
FastGen [14] selects per-head compression policies from ob-
served attention patterns, adapting the operator while staying at

All of these methods score importance at the (layer, head)
or (layer, token) granularity. Under shared buffers, indepen-
dent head-level selections inflate memory by up to 𝑟× unless
a budget allocator compensates—which is the compensation
Ada-KV supplies—and per-head scores cannot be materialized
without unrolling the cache. Page-EntroKV attacks the inflation
at its source: pooling before selection makes the union seman-
tics of GQA harmless by construction, and eviction runs at page
granularity.
A concurrent entropy-based method, EntroKV [18], applies
attention entropy at a different decision point. There, Shannon
entropy allocates per-layer compression budgets—how much
each layer keeps—as a plug-and-play front-end, with offline
calibration of a mean-entropy statistic ¯H. Page-EntroKV ap-
plies sink-isolated Rényi-2 entropy on the selection side—
which pages survive within each physical group—requires no
calibration, and carries the guarantees proved here: group-
level UOR ≡1 (Theorem 3) and finite-context needle retention
(Corollary 2). The two mechanisms sit at different points in the
pipeline (budget allocation versus within-group selection); no
combination of the two is studied here. Table 1 positions Page-
EntroKV against these families, and against channel-dimension
and routing alternatives, along the axes that the GQA layout
makes salient.
2.4. Mechanistic head specialization
Pruning studies showed early that many attention heads are
redundant [25, 26]. Retrieval heads carry long-context factu-
ality mechanistically [24]. DuoAttention [19] statically classi-
fies retrieval versus streaming heads and applies heterogeneous
compression—but it classifies offline, at the granularity of the
attention computation, and needs a calibration corpus. Page-
EntroKV weights heads within each physical GQA group at
runtime, using measured sink-isolated entropy, needs no cali-
bration, and preserves the physical sharing structure of GQA.
3. Problem formulation and background
We first fix notation (Table A.8 in Section Appendix A), then
characterize the decode-phase memory wall, the physical layout
of GQA serving engines, and the structural metrics that quantify
why head-independent eviction fails.
3.1. The decode-phase memory wall
Decoding runs in two distinct execution regimes.
Pre-
fill processes the input prompt via compute-bound matrix–
matrix products. Autoregressive decoding emits one token per
step: self-attention over historical tokens streams the key–value
(KV) cache from high-bandwidth memory (HBM) into on-chip
SRAM, reducing the computation to memory-bound matrix–
vector products.

4.3. Systems integration in vLLM
Page-EntroKV never materializes 𝐻𝑄× 𝑇intermediate at-
tention matrices in global DRAM. Entropy estimation, Boltz-
mann weighting, and page reduction are fused into two kernels
executing in on-chip SRAM (Fig. 3). Kernel 1 computes QK
dot-products, sink-isolated collision entropies, and Boltzmann
weights in FP16 registers, then performs the group reduction
in shared memory. Kernel 2 applies the block-table mapping,
runs the max-reduction per physical page, and performs two-
pass radix top-𝐾𝑙selection, emitting the retention mask with-
out DRAM materialization. Unreferenced blocks return to the
vLLM free-page pool immediately. Section Appendix D gives
the PyTorch reference implementation.

Remark 3 (Scope of the assumptions and boundary cases).
Theorem 4 gives a sufficient condition under which entropy-
weighted pooling provably dominates arithmetic mean pooling:
siblings must have near-maximal sink-isolated entropy. Three
boundary cases delimit it. First, the needle is assumed non-sink:
𝑡∗∉𝑆sink excludes a needle inside the sink region—the first
|𝑆sink| prompt positions—which the theorem does not cover.
Second, the result extends to multiple retrieval heads: pooling
preserves the convex combination of their masses rather than
a single head’s mass; siblings with intermediate entropy tran-
sition smoothly per Proposition 2. Third, when every head in
a group has near-identical high entropy, no head satisfies the
retrieval-head premise; the weights degenerate toward uniform
(Proposition 2 ii), and the max-over-heads fallback of Section 7
is the designated mitigation.

6. Empirical validation: pilot results and pre-registered
protocol
This paper’s guarantees are of two kinds, and the evidence
differs accordingly. The architectural guarantees—UOR ≡1
under pooling (Theorem 3) and the per-layer union bounds
(Proposition 4)—hold by construction. Everything else requires
measurement. This section reports a pilot validation executed
against the pre-registered protocol, discloses every deviation
from it, and marks precisely which protocol items remain pend-
ing. Two governance principles govern all reporting. First,
claims follow tables: the abstract, introduction, and conclusion
assert only what a table, figure, or theorem supports. Second,
aggregates are recomputable: every summary below is tran-
scribed from the released per-cell logs.
6.1. Pilot setup and measurement governance
The pilot runs on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (𝐿=28, 𝐻𝑄=12,
𝐻KV=2, 𝑟=6) in native FP16, loaded with eager attention
so that per-head probability vectors are exposed for entropy
computation—the capture requirement the protocol already an-
ticipates, since fused kernels do not expose attention distribu-
tions. Pages are simulated over the model’s cache with logical
blocking at 𝐵=16. Hardware: a single NVIDIA T4 GPU; seeds
are fixed per run and recorded in the released logs.
The pre-registered Phase 1 names Llama-3.1-8B (𝑟=4) un-
der vLLM on an A100-80 GB with contexts to 128k. The pilot
deviates on four points, stated explicitly: (i) model: Qwen2.5-
1.5B (𝑟=6) for single-GPU capture feasibility; (ii) runtime:
HuggingFace transformers with eager attention, page quotas
simulated at 𝐵=16 rather than read from a vLLM block man-
ager; (iii) scale: contexts 𝑇∈[0.9k, 8.2k] across the measure-
ment suite, needle-recall horizons ≤2.5k, and budgets 𝑘/𝑇∈
{0.02, . . . , 0.40} rather than the 4k–128k sweep; (iv) serving:
no vLLM serving measurements yet.
The pilot is therefore
a validation tier of the structural core, not the pre-registered
sweep.
Three rules were applied to every table below. Coverage
certification: the certified ablation run covers 56 of 56 layer–
group pairs (100%) with zero NaN-skipped layers, so ev-

6.4. Retrieval and downstream feasibility
Table 6 reports needle retention under two success criteria,
because they answer different questions and were measured in
separate runs.
Under the pooled-selection criterion—a trial
counts as retained if the needle’s page survives the pooled-
selection eviction decision, with no requirement across layers—
entropy-weighted pooling retains the needle at 100% across all
pilot contexts and depths while arithmetic-mean pooling retains
it at 0%: the dilution mechanism of Eq. (19) is directly observ-
able. Under the strict all-layer criterion—the needle’s page
must survive in every layer—both methods fail at depths 25–
75% (weighted recall 0–33% across strict variants; mean pool-
ing 0% throughout), and both retain trivially at depth 100%,
where the needle sits in the final pages that the quota never
evicts. Every zero cell was verified as a genuine eviction: in
30 of 30 zero cells the needle page’s rank fell below the re-
tention quota in the layer that dropped it. The uncompressed
cache retains the needle at all depths under both criteria, so the
failures are budget effects, not harness artifacts.

7. Discussion and limitations
Failure modes.. When all heads in a group exhibit near-
identical high entropy (uniformly diffuse attention), the Boltz-
mann weights degenerate toward uniform (Proposition 2 ii),
and Page-EntroKV reduces to mean pooling—re-opening the
needle-dilution risk (Remark 3). The fallback sanctioned by
Proposition 2 (iii) is to switch a group to max-over-heads pool-
ing when intra-group entropy variance falls below a threshold;
the switch is cost-neutral for the budget guarantee, which The-
orem 3 ties only to pooling before selection.
Pilot limitations.. Three limitations scope the empirical tier.
First, strict all-layer needle retention fails at pilot scale at
depths 25–75%: the premise of Theorem 4 holds directionally
but not in its strong regime, so pooled needle mass (∼10−3)
cannot top a 20% quota in every layer; the asymptotic guaran-
tee and deployed budgets are reconciled only at Phase-1 scale
(Section 6.5), and the two-regime check of Fig. C.9 delimits
exactly where the guarantee binds. Second, needle-recall hori-
zons are ≤2.5k tokens; the 4k–128k sweep is pending. Third,
the pilot covers a single model family (Qwen2.5-1.5B, 𝑟=6);
cross-family generality is deferred to Phase 1, and no serving-
level measurements exist yet.
Kernel overhead.. The reference implementation computes en-
tropy, weights, pooling, and page reduction as separate oper-
ations.
The two-kernel fused design of Section 4.3—warp-
level dot products for sink-isolated collision entropy, register-
resident weighting, page-by-page block-max, and in-kernel
radix top-𝐾selection (Proposition 5 and Remarks 5 and 6)—
is what reaches the throughput characteristics the framework is
designed for; its open-source release is future work.
Runtime configuration dependency.. The framework’s guaran-
tees are stated relative to the engine’s block size and page ta-
ble: 𝐾𝑙scales with 1/𝐵(Eq. (23)), the page-level union ratio
is bounded by 𝐻KV (Proposition 4), and selection cost scales
with 𝑇/𝐵(Proposition 5). Deployments must pin the engine
version and block size together with the eviction configuration;
the protocol records both with every log.

Orthogonality to KV-cache quantization.. Page-EntroKV op-
erates exclusively along the sequence dimension 𝑇, selecting
which pages to retain; low-bit KV quantization (KIVI [21],
QuaRot [22]) operates along the channel dimension, reducing
the bytes per stored element from 2 (FP16) to 0.5 (INT4). The
two optimizations compose multiplicatively: a 20% page bud-
get yields a 5× reduction along 𝑇by construction, and INT4
yields a further 4× along the element axis—20× combined rel-
ative to the uncompressed FP16 cache. The pooling and selec-
tion logic is unaffected by storage precision: importance scores
are computed from attention probabilities during the QK dot
product, which are accumulated in floating point regardless of
the stored representation; quantization changes only the stored
K and V tensors. This is a design-level composition argument,
not a measured deployment result.

does not transfer directly to latent-compression designs such as
multi-head latent attention [32], where KV states are collapsed
into a shared latent vector and no per-token page structure ex-
ists to evict. For MHA (𝑟= 1) the union bound is vacuous;

8. Conclusion
We presented Page-EntroKV, a formal framework for
hardware-aligned KV-cache eviction that resolves the mismatch
between head-independent eviction policies and physically
shared GQA buffers. The framework formalizes the union over-
head ratio and intra-group disagreement, proves their exact ana-
lytic relationship at 𝑟= 2, and bounds the inflation two-sidedly
at every group ratio; it replaces head-level selection with sink-
isolated entropy-weighted pooling at the physical group level;
and it projects importance onto the discrete page frames that
serving engines allocate, so eviction executes exactly at the
tuple (𝑙, 𝑔, 𝑃𝑏) with strict, provable budget preservation and
finite-context needle retention. A pilot validation on Qwen2.5-
1.5B-Instruct confirms the structural core on real text—heavy-
tailed union inflation with envelope-certified overhead, sink-
isolation ordering inversion, exact pooled cardinality, and the
certified temperature and Rényi-order characterizations—while
the full-scale sweep proceeds under the pre-registered protocol.

leasing the fused kernels, entropy-based inter-layer budget al-
