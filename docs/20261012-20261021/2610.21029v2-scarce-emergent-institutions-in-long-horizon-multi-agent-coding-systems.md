## 摘要
随着自主智能体被广泛部署，协调对共享计算资源与服务的访问将日益重要。近期事件已揭示智能体之间存在非计划协调，凸显了这些交互的安全影响。然而，以往关于智能体协商的研究常使用风格化博弈和模拟议价，未能说明这些行为在持续的长时程任务中如何发展。我们提出 SCARCE（Shared Compute ARena for Coding agEnts，编码智能体共享计算竞技场），一个让智能体在各自截止期限内完成一系列编码任务、同时协商共享执行资源访问权的框架。这种协商决定它们何时以及多长时间能够检查程序、编译代码和运行测试。我们发现智能体会自发形成非正式制度：基于声誉、互惠和先例的持久规则与承诺。我们将这些安排称为制度行为，它们会跨任务延续并影响资源分配，智能体有时会放弃即时优势以遵守先前协议。我们建立了包含21个类别的协商策略与涌现规范分类体系，并用其分析八个模型，审计不同环境设置下的1500余条轨迹。在测试的模型中，较新的前沿模型比更早或轻量模型表现出更多制度行为。除合作外，我们还观察到排他性联盟和少见的分而治之策略。SCARCE 提供了一个受控环境，用于前瞻性、系统地审计这些关系如何涌现、如何塑造资源获取，以及如何影响多智能体系统的安全与协调。

## Abstract
As autonomous agents become widely deployed, coordinating access to shared computing resources and services will become increasingly important. Recent incidents have already revealed unplanned coordination between agents, highlighting the safety implications of these interactions. Yet prior studies of agent negotiation often use stylized games and simulated bargaining, leaving open how these behaviors develop during sustained, long-horizon tasks. We propose SCARCE (Shared Compute ARena for Coding agEnts), a framework in which agents solve sequences of coding tasks under individual deadlines while negotiating access to a shared execution resource. This negotiation determines when and for how long they can inspect programs, compile code, and run tests. We find that agents spontaneously develop informal institutions: persistent rules and commitments grounded in reputation, reciprocity, and precedent. These arrangements, which we call institutional behaviors, carry across tasks and influence resource allocation, with agents sometimes forgoing immediate advantages to honor earlier agreements. We develop a 21-category taxonomy of negotiation strategies and emergent norms and use it to analyze eight models and audit more than 1,500 trajectories across different environmental settings. Among the models tested, newer frontier models exhibit more institutional behaviors than older or lightweight models. Alongside cooperation, we observe exclusionary coalitions and rare divide-and-conquer strategies. SCARCE provides a controlled setting for proactively and systematically auditing how these relationships emerge, shape access to resources, and affect the safety and coordination of multi-agent systems.
