# Close Enough is Nowhere Near: Fidelity-Consumability Decoupling in KV Caches 

# Adam Deng MIT ORC Shicheng Liu MIT CSAIL Yada Zhu IBM Research 

Abstract 

Multi-agent LLM systems communicate in text, which is lossy and wasteful: the sender throws away the reading it already did. A growing literature instead transmits the KV cache, the natural carrier of that reading. We study the most appealing form of the idea: both models stay frozen and the sender’s cache is substituted for the reader’s with nothing trained. It does not work, and the obstruction belongs to the reader. A frozen transformer reads only caches close to the one it would have written itself, a region we call its tolerable set. We show that geometric similarity to that cache does not predict whether the reader can use it, and the failure is not gradual, so a more faithful substitute is not the remedy it appears to be. We explain this phenomenon with competitive attention. Experimentally, we test eight diverse substitution methods against two measured anchors: an uninformative-cache baseline and the reader’s own accuracy from having read the passage. All sit at the baseline. That turns a long tail of separate negative results into one outcome with a stated condition for overturning it. A different design does work: if the reader keeps its own cache and attends over the sender’s as extra context, adapting only its queries, it beats its own baseline, and also clears the baseline with eight senders pooled where undirected attention over the same union falls below it. We offer this as a characterization on one probe, not a leaderboard claim. Our central contribution is a redirection: pursuing faithful substitute caches is the wrong target; adding a cache to what the reader already has is the promising one. 

# 1 Introduction and Related Work 

Multi-agent language-model systems communicate through generated text (Wang et al., 2024; Du et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023; Hong et al., 2024; Xi et al., 2023), at a structural cost: the sender discards the reading it already performed by projecting it into tokens, the reader pays for that reading again, and whatever did not survive the projection into words is unrecoverable. That bottleneck motivates transmitting the internal state computed during reading instead. A growing literature pursues it through cache-to-cache messaging over a learned gated residual (Fu et al., 2026), trained latent alignment of key–value memories (Dery et al., 2026), and closed-form cache maps within a model family (Heo et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026). The key–value (KV) cache is the natural candidate carrier for such a channel. A transformer constructs one during prefill regardless of whether anything is shared, so the representation is already paid for. It records what the model read in digested form instead of in words, and it already resides in memory, addressable and of known shape. This raises a question prior to the design of any particular channel: what will a frozen model accept from another model in place of its own cache? We study the most permissive version of the proposal: both models frozen, the sender’s cache handed to the reader in place of its own, no access for the sender to how the reader reads, and no fine-tuning. This is the version that composes, since any two frozen agents will do, with no joint training and no representational conventions negotiated in advance. It fails, and it fails for a reason that belongs to the receiving model, not to any particular bridge. Scattered reports of such failures exist, typically attributed to the limitations of one construction. What we find instead is a property of the reader itself: a frozen transformer reads a cache correctly only if that cache lies inside a narrow neighborhood of the one its own forward pass would have produced, and it is narrow in a sense that no norm on the cache captures. We call that neighborhood the reader’s tolerable set , and we find that nothing constructed from the sender’s side lands inside it. Two findings are worth stating at this level of generality, because both run against the prevailing intuition. Geometric similarity to the reader’s own cache does not predict whether the reader can use it: we exhibit pairs ordered one way by similarity and the opposite way by transfer. The failure is also not a matter of 1arXiv:2610.22016v1 [cs.LG] 15 Oct 2026 accumulation over a deep stack: corrupting a single layer is already as damaging as corrupting all twenty-eight. The mechanism is located at the point where a cache enters the reader’s computation. That point is the attention softmax, and it is competitive: keys are scored against each other, not against a threshold, so the output is determined by which tokens prevail. A displacement too small to register as geometric distance suffices to change which tokens prevail, after which the reader attends to the wrong part of its context. Section 4 measures this directly, and also rules out the alternative account on which off-manifold caches are catastrophically amplified. This diagnosis suggests a different design. Rather than replacing the reader’s cache, retain it and expose the sender’s cache as additional attendable context, with adaptation confined to the reader’s query side. The reader is then never asked to consume a foreign object in place of its own, and the tolerable set does not bind. We show that this substantially improves transfer, can match the reader’s native-context performance, and continues to work when several senders are pooled. 

One distinction organizes the prior work. Results that look inconsistent in isolation reconcile once three cases are separated: whether a shared cache must replace the reader’s own (substitution), is merely added to what the reader attends over (augmentation), or comes from a map fitted against the reader’s own caches. Sections 2 and 5 state these as claims (C1)–(C3). The internal-channel proposals above divide along exactly this axis, and the strongest of them supports rather than contradicts our account: the cache-to-cache ablation that projects the sender’s cache into the receiver’s slots collapses, while the configuration keeping the receiver’s cache and adding to it is the one that works. It also explains why text worked. Text re-enters through the receiver’s own encoder before any attention occurs, and a borrowed cache is afforded no such entry; retrieval-style cross-attention (Borgeaud et al., 2022) and fusion of model 

outputs (Wang et al., 2024; Du et al., 2023) are augmentation on the same reading, and the division predicts that they work, as they do. Two adjacent literatures fall on the near side of the distinction. Same-model compression shrinks a cache by evicting low-attention tokens (Zhang et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023), factorizing across depth (Liu et al., 2024), or quantizing entries (Hooper et al., 2024). These methods succeed, and our account is consistent with that because producer and reader are the same model there, so every modification perturbs a cache the reader itself wrote and the question is asked from inside the tolerable set rather than from outside it (Section 4). Work reusing caches across layers or models (Liu et al., 2024; 2026) or merging weights to pool capabilities (Wan et al., 2024) reports mixed outcomes the distinction predicts: where reuse is substitution and the bridge is built without reference to the reader’s own cache on the target context, we find it held to the accuracy the reader already reaches without any shared cache, and the binding constraint belongs to the reader rather than the bridge (Section 3.3). A separate line measures correspondence between representations and exploits it (Kornblith et al., 2019; Moschella et al., 2023; Bansal et al., 2021), acknowledging but stating weakly that similarity is not interchangeability. Our results sharpen that caveat and locate its reason, for attention, in the gap between the Euclidean alignment such indices measure and the competitive readout that consumes a cache (Sections 3.1 and 4). 

Fitted bridges, and our claim’s boundary. A bridge fitted against the reader’s own caches, regressed onto the very quantity it must reproduce, is different from the sender-side constructions we test. Our negative claim is scoped away from it: we run no fitted bridge, and state that as a non-claim. Published evidence makes this a real boundary. Heo et al. (2026) fit per-head closed-form ridge maps between members of one model family, freeze both models, and have the receiver decode from the mapped cache in place of its own, retaining most of the original quality; Dery et al. (2026) train a translator constructing the receiver’s prefix cache from the sender’s, with large gains over an untrained identity map. Both satisfy the qualification our claim carries: the map is fitted against the reader’s forward pass on the transferred context rather than built from the sender’s side alone, which establishes that the qualification is not idle; it is the whole content of the claim. Both settings evaluate under redundancy instead of information asymmetry, the receiver being scored on continuations of a context it has seen or could recompute; our probe is built to remove that redundancy, and we claim only that a fitted within-family map has, to our knowledge, not been tested there (Appendix J). 

# 2 The Object of Study 

The object of study is not a cache, but what a particular frozen model does with one: supply it with key and value tensors, let attention consume them in place of recomputing them, and read the output distribution. Everything downstream is fixed, the weights being fixed. The consequential point, and the origin of every difficulty that follows, is that this 2mapping belongs to the reader. One cache supplied to two models passes through two different functions, and a value computed by one carries no particular meaning for the other. One cache is distinguished: 𝐶 ∗, the cache the reader itself produces on the true context, calibrated by construction because the weights that wrote it are the weights now reading it. How far from 𝐶 ∗ may a cache lie and still be read correctly? The caches so satisfying are the reader’s tolerable set , and cache sharing is the problem of placing a candidate inside it. That set has positive radius and is strongly anisotropic (Section 4), so claims of narrowness below concern reachability by the methods we test rather than a singleton. Geometric closeness to 𝐶 ∗ admits several standard measures, cosine similarity, reconstruction error, and attention-logit correlation, and we call any of them the candidate’s fidelity .Whether the reader can consume the cache is a distinct predicate, consumability ; the two come apart (Section 3.1). Throughout, transfer names the dependent variable: the fraction of probe items on which the reader’s greedy continuation contains the answer string (Appendix A). On-manifold names an object a real forward pass of this reader could have produced, which in practice means a key it did produce on some context. A construction landing in the column space of 

𝑊 𝐾 satisfies a weaker condition, named explicitly where it arises and never called manifold membership. Two anchors, measured within each run and not assumed, frame every number. The floor , 0 .180, is the accuracy from the reader’s own cache with the answer masked out, the rate at which the question is answerable from the rest of the passage. The native-cache reference , 0 .665, is the accuracy from its own cache of the unmasked context. Neither is a formal bound, so values outside [0.180 , 0.665 ] are possible and are flagged where they occur. Our position: (C1) Sender-side substitution is floor-bounded. A method that constructs a cache and installs it in the reader’s slots in place of 𝐶 ∗ stays near the floor unless its output lands inside the tolerable set, and raising fidelity does not help. The scope is methods built from the sender’s side alone , without fitting the producing map against the reader’s own forward pass on the context being transferred; every arm of our sweep observes that discipline. (C2) Augmentation is not bound the same way. A method that leaves 𝐶 ∗ in place, appends the sender’s cache as further attendable context, and adapts only the query side never asks the reader to consume a foreign cache in place of its own. It falls outside the regime the tolerable set constrains and can approach the native-cache reference. Both claims admit refutation, and Section 6 states the refuting observation for each alongside the experiments most likely to produce it. 

# 3 Three Measurements of the Tolerable Set 

Every measurement in this section uses one reader and one probe. The reader is Qwen2.5-1.5B (Qwen Team, 2024), 28 layers with grouped-query attention at group size 𝐺 =6, frozen throughout. The probe is split-context factual recall built from SQuAD: a sender has read a passage containing the answer, the reader receives the same passage with the answer masked together with the question, and the reader is supplied a cache derived from the sender and scored on whether it produces the answer. Decoding is greedy over eight tokens at 𝑛 =200 items unless stated otherwise, and the full specification with both anchors is Appendix A. The three measurements ask how faithful a cache must be, how much of it must be correct, and whether selecting a real cache instead of synthesizing one evades the constraint. 

# 3.1 Cosine Similarity Does Not Predict Consumability 

The most direct formulation displaces the reader’s own cache by a calibrated distance and finds where the reader fails. The content is the reader’s own masked cache, which carries no answer, so tolerance of the geometry alone would register at the floor. Tolerance turns out to be minimal. At cosine 0 .9999 accuracy sits at the floor, 0 .180; at 0 .999 it has fallen to 0 .120; at 0 .995 it reaches 0 .005 and stays there for every smaller cosine tested. That last value warrants attention, being not the floor but far below it: a cache 99 .5% aligned with the reader’s own leaves it performing worse than one carrying no information at all, having destroyed its use of the context still available to it. What makes this a claim about the instrument rather than about noise is the comparison against a genuine cache, the one this same frozen model produces on the unmasked passage. At full strength it sits at achieved cosine 0 .9949, further 

from the reader’s own cache than the noise condition scoring 0 .005, and it transfers at 0 .445 (Figure 1(a), Table B). Cosine does not predict consumability weakly; it orders these two conditions backwards. The governing property is closer to provenance than to distance: what matters is whether a real forward pass of a model with this geometry 3produced the object, and no norm-based metric detects that. The methodological consequence bears on a substantial literature, where reconstruction error and cosine are standard instruments for evaluating cache reconstruction, compression, and sharing. On this evidence they can be driven arbitrarily close to their optima while the capability they proxy is absent, and they can rank a working method below a broken one. A transfer result reported in cosine or mean-squared error alone is uninformative as to whether the intended reader can consume it. Such metrics remain useful as diagnostics, and we use them as such, but behavioral evaluation on the reader that will consume the cache is what settles the question. We show this for one reader and offer it as a recommendation, not a proven universal. 

Sparsity of the perturbation. Production methods reproduce most features approximately and a few badly, and attention might route around a few corrupted coordinates. Neither form of the objection survives: the layer experiment below is the structural analogue, holding 27 of 28 layers bit-exact, and pairing each sparse condition with a dense perturbation at matched achieved cosine, the two agree throughout (Appendix H). The result above needs no narrowing to dense perturbations. 

# 3.2 A Single Corrupted Layer of Twenty-Eight 

The second measurement concerns how much of a cache must be correct. We corrupt the first ℓ layers and take layers 

ℓ+1 through 28 unchanged from the reader’s own forward pass, sweeping ℓ from 0 to 28 under two corruptions at matched mean key cosine 0 .822: isotropic noise, which no forward pass would produce, and replacement by the nearest genuine key from a bank the reader generated on other passages. The off-manifold curve of Figure 2(a) carries the result. One corrupted layer takes the reader from 0 .665 to 0 .030, while the remaining 27 layers stay bit-identical to what it wrote, not approximations of it, and that counts for nothing. The curve is flat from ℓ=1 to ℓ=28: the damage is done at once and does not accumulate with depth. What this excludes should be stated directly, the compounding account being a natural reading of the failure and a widely held one. Were the mechanism a small per-layer error with survival factor 𝜋 < 1 raised to the depth, one corrupted layer would cost about 𝜋 and the curve would bend downward as ℓ grew. That predicts a gradual slope; the measurement is a step function, so the account is discarded rather than qualified. The on-manifold row supplies the control, behaving at matched cosine exactly as compounding describes: one genuine substituted layer is survivable and slightly beneficial at 0 .695, three layers reach 0 .745, decay sets in around ℓ=8, and zero arrives only once all twenty-eight are replaced. Depth-wise accumulation is therefore real and observable in this sweep, and it is not what destroys a borrowed cache. Two corruptions identical in fidelity, differing only in whether a real forward pass could have produced the injected object, diverge from fatal within one layer to surviving eight. The distinguishing property is neither geometric distance nor depth. To check whether this reflects the reader’s particular depth, we ran the substitution test across seven models spanning 6 to 36 layers: Pythia-70m/410m/1.4b, Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B/3B, and GPT-2. Full substitution by nearest genuine keys transfers at or below 0 .008 in every case, at 𝑛 =120 against each model’s own anchors. The shallowest reader bounds the claim without confirming it, its own native-cache reference leaving almost no headroom, so the informative cases run from twelve to thirty-six layers (Appendix C). A shallower reader is not more permissive, which is the expected outcome if the failure occurs at a consumption point present in every layer. 

# 3.3 Selection from Six Thousand Genuine Keys 

Both preceding measurements construct a cache, which invites the response that construction is the difficulty: any synthesized object will be subtly wrong in consequential ways, so the operation should instead be selection . Choosing from a library of real caches guarantees the result lies on the reader’s own manifold, the reader having produced every candidate. The library holds six thousand genuine keys per layer, harvested from this reader’s forward passes over other passages; for each slot we retrieve the 𝑘 -th nearest and interpolate toward it with weight 𝛼 , so that at 𝛼 =1 every key attended over is a real key the reader produced on a different passage. Selection provides no relief (Figure 1(b); construction and full 𝑘 × 𝛼 grid in Appendix D). The nearest genuine key, the closest real object among six thousand, transfers 0 .000, and by 𝛼 =0.5 already 0 .005. Sweeping the rank out to 𝑘 =16 4leaves the pattern unchanged, so no graded reward for proximity exists among on-manifold candidates. This is our sharpest statement of how narrow the set is, and it should be distinguished from a claim with which it is often conflated. Manifold membership, in the sense fixed in Section 2, is not the operative property: every candidate here is a key this reader actually produced, and none is consumable. The reader does not need a point of the right kind, and a nearby point will not do either, the nearest of 6,000 genuine keys being already too far. Membership is necessary and far from sufficient. A broader version of the same idea reinforces the reading. Nine variants crossing three codebook constructions, the real-key bank, a learned vector-quantized codebook, and a PCA lattice, with three snapping rules, yield a best cell at the highest cosine fidelity we achieved by pure selection, 0 .880, with attention-logit correlation 0.928. Yet this best cell transfers 0 .000, despite having a higher cosine similarity. Among selection methods fidelity and consumability come apart and then run against each other.

# 4 Why Substitution Cannot Reach the Tolerable Set 

Three measurements converge on one property. This section accounts for it, locating the failure not in the cache but in the operation that consumes it. We begin with the explanation one reaches for first. 

# 4.1 Not Amplification 

The intuitive account posits a cliff: off-manifold displacements are amplified catastrophically en route to the output while on-manifold ones are not. It is testable, and it is wrong. Measured at both stages through which an injected key acts, the off-/on-manifold readout gain is an 𝑂 (1) ratio, moderately more consequential and not catastrophically so, with no layer exhibiting a blowup and the logit stage attenuating instead of amplifying (Figure 2(b); measurement in Appendix E). The sensitivity that does exist is architectural and analytically pinned, its governing constant set by the grouped-query attention (Ainslie et al., 2023) group size: several query heads read each key slot, so a displacement there is read out once per head. That factor is real, and far too small to account for the collapse. Whatever destroys a borrowed cache does not do so by enlarging the displacement. 54.2 The Softmax Consumption Point

A displacement far too small to register geometrically can flip a decisive contest, while a larger one along an uncontested direction passes unnoticed. This is the explanation, and the rest follows from it. The contests are also numerous, one per query, per head, and per layer, so a modest context stages tens of thousands at once. Tolerance means surviving every one. Three measurements are consistent with the account and hard to reconcile with its alternatives. At matched cosine, a genuine cache is consumable and a noise-calibrated one is not, so what matters is whether the displacement respects the margin structure the reader’s own forward passes produce, and not how far it moves (Section 3.1). Manifold membership fares no better: it constrains where a key lies, not which contest it wins, and a genuine key is genuine for some other context (Section 3.3). Nor does anything compound over depth, one layer’s attention already being a competition whose outcome the intact layers downstream faithfully process (Section 3.2). 

The tolerance is anisotropic, not uniformly small. This formulation best fits the evidence, and it reconciles our negative result with a literature of positive ones. Tolerance is wide along directions the reader’s attention does not resolve and thin along those it does. A norm on the cache averages over both and reports neither. Same-model compression, whether by eviction, factorization, or low-bit quantization, operates on a cache the reader itself wrote, and in succeeding demonstrates that the wide directions really are wide. Cross-model production has no reason to respect the orientation, and lands in the thin ones. The open question is therefore not how to make a produced cache closer, but how to confine its residual error to directions the reader cannot resolve, and nothing in our sweep suggests the sender’s side has the information required. Fragility is thus a property of the readout, not of the representation or of the stack. It is the generic consequence of handing a candidate object to a normalized competitive operator. A KV cache is reader-specific, portable only through a mechanism with access to how the reader will consume it, a qualification Section 1 shows is doing real work. The account predicts one asymmetry, and it holds: keys set the competition while values are averaged under weights already fixed, so key damage costs more. At matched sparsity, destroying thirteen of two hundred fifty-six value coordinates is free while the same damage to keys is fatal, and cosine orders the two backwards (Appendix H). The account also supplies a design lever, developed next. If a foreign cache cannot survive consumption in place of the reader’s own, it should not be put there. 

# 5 Consequences: Two Regimes Run and a Third Surveyed 

The characterization predicts a division, not a gradient, and the sweep below tests it by varying how the shared cache is produced and where it is placed , holding the reader, the probe, and the anchors fixed. Under substitution the produced cache occupies the reader’s own key/value slots. Under augmentation the reader keeps its own and attends over the sender’s as additional context. Substitution must land inside the tolerable set and should therefore be floor-bounded however it is built from the sender’s side alone. Augmentation never faces that requirement, and since the reader ends with strictly more to attend over, it should be able to reach the native-cache reference. A third regime lies outside the sweep: a fitted bridge , whose producing map is regressed against the reader’s own caches, not built from the sender’s side. We state it as a non-claim, that (C3) fitted bridges are not floor-bounded, and we do not run one . # 5.2 Augmentation Clears the Floor and Reaches the Native-Cache Reference 

Changing only the placement, the reader retains its own cache 𝐶 ∗ and attends over the concatenation [𝐶 ∗; 𝐶 sender ], with the sender’s cache passed through verbatim. There is no reconstruction, no projection, and no bridge trained toward resemblance. With no trained parameters this transfers at 0 .555, three times the floor and beyond the reach of the entire substitution family. Adapting the reader’s query-side projections with LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) at rank 16 raises it to 0.695, at the native-cache reference of 0 .665. At 𝑛 =200 that crossing is six items ( 𝑧 = 0.64), so we report it as reaching the reference, not as beating it. The comparison that carries weight is against the floor, where the zero-parameter arm alone stands at 𝑧 = 7.8. The gap is not a single-variable contrast, and we do not present it as one. Substitution removes 𝐶 ∗ while augmentation keeps it and adds to it, so the augmented reader has strictly more to attend over, and part of the gap follows from that and not from placement alone. The closest matched contrast we have is the genuine-cache arm of Appendix B: an answer-bearing cache this same model wrote on the unmasked passage reaches 0 .445 substituted into the slots, against 0.555 for the same kind of object supplied as attendable context. Both arms carry the answer, so the comparison holds content roughly fixed and varies placement, and the placement is worth more than the fidelity. The oracle and augmentation arms are not the same object in two locations, the bank key carrying no answer, so we rest nothing on that pair beyond the provenance point. We note two qualifications. (1) The strongest arm carries the least clean negative control, and control-subtracted the keys-and-values variant slightly exceeds it, so the ordering among adapted variants should not be relied upon (Appendix F); what survives the controls is the regime gap, an order of magnitude larger. (2) Sender and reader are the same frozen checkpoint here, reading different contexts (Appendix A), so this is placement within one model and not communication between two. 

# 5.3 Scaling the Sender Pool 

A division holding for a single sender is of limited use to a multi-agent system, so we scaled the pool: the reader attends over the union of 𝑁 senders’ caches, exactly one of which has read the passage containing the answer (Appendix G). The bridge is trained at 𝑁 =4 alone, so 𝑁 =1 and 𝑁 =2 are transfers out of that condition and 𝑁 =8 is extrapolation beyond it. Figure 3(b) shows the two arms diverging as the pool grows, and the full table is Appendix G. Undirected attention over a union degrades rapidly and falls below the floor by four senders, the distractors dominating. The adapted bridge decays gradually instead, from 0 .785 at 𝑁 =1 to 0 .395 at 𝑁 =8, still clearing the floor at eight senders with a control-subtracted lift of +0.165. We report control-subtracted values because the controls climb with 𝑁 , a larger pool admitting more scoring by chance, and the runs at 𝑁 =4 and 𝑁 =8 flagged their own controls as elevated. What the two arms establish is that query-side adaptation is what keeps a pooled cache useful as 𝑁 grows. We did not measure where the adapted bridge places its attention, so we do not claim selectivity as the route. 85.4 What the Sweep Licenses 

The division between the two regimes dominates every other source of variation: where the cache is placed decides the outcome, and how well it was built does not. Capacity, objective, and even a guarantee that the produced cache lies on the reader’s own manifold leave substitution where it started, and the fine structure inside each regime is immaterial against the distance between them. We do not claim the regimes differ in kind as a matter of principle, for the reason given above. The design rule follows from the mechanism and not from a benchmark result. A cache should not be reconstructed, selected, or synthesized to stand in for another model’s own. It should be supplied as context and permitted to re-enter through the reader’s own query pathway.

# 6 Limitations and Falsification 

Scope. All numbers we report derive from one reader and one instrument: a split-context factual-recall probe. The anchors and sensitivity constants are that reader’s properties, and the anchors are measured baselines, not proven bounds. The probe is also a single kind of task, and its character may matter as much as its identity: short factual recall turns on whether one span wins attention, plausibly the condition most sensitive to the margin reordering we identify as the mechanism. Tasks spreading their dependence across many tokens may be more forgiving. Our claims are scoped accordingly, to a frozen reader, sender-side construction, and short factual recall under information asymmetry, with the depth ladder the only axis on which the reader varies. Augmentation results are same-architecture. Across families they fail at dimension matching, so we make no cross-family claim. 

Falsifiers. (C1) fails if some substitution method transfers well above the floor while its output demonstrably lies outside the tolerable set, severing consumability from membership. (C2) fails if augmentation with query-side adaptation cannot clear the floor. (C3) marks a boundary, not a prediction: a bridge fitted by regression against the reader’s own caches is not claimed to be floor-bounded, and we do not run one. Because we attribute the wall to the softmax and not to depth, raising readout sharpness should contract the tolerable set while adding layers at fixed sharpness should not, the two being ordinarily confounded and worth varying independently. A reader consuming its cache somewhere other than a softmax should also be more permissive, and tolerance should widen as a task’s answer depends less sharply on individual attention margins. The sparsity of the perturbation (Section 3.1) has been tested at the feature level and does not favor the objection (Appendix H); the sharpest outstanding test is the carrier comparison noted below. 

A prediction the account makes. If the obstruction is a competitive normalized readout, a carrier consumed before 

any softmax should prove markedly more permissive. Residual-stream activations are such a carrier, read through normalization, a linear map, and an additive residual. Our preliminary work with them is consistent with markedly greater tolerance, by roughly the margin the mechanism would predict (numbers and caveats in Appendix I). We report it as a direction, not a result, because those runs carry their own anchors and compare across sharing protocols instead 9of holding displacement matched. The controlled version is the sharpest way to falsify the mechanism: if a pre-softmax carrier proved no more permissive than keys, the account would be wrong. 

Conclusion. A frozen transformer reads a cache handed to it in place of its own only when that cache is very nearly the one it would have written itself. The tolerable set has positive radius, but that radius is not what any fidelity metric reports, and it is small along exactly the directions attention resolves. What this excludes is replacement. What it leaves is augmentation, which reframes the motivating objective. Multi-agent systems need one model to attend over another’s state, not to overwrite its own with it. Internal communication between frozen models is not blocked in principle. It is blocked when posed as replacement under a sender-side construction discipline.

# A Probe and Anchors 

The probe is split-context factual recall built from SQuAD. For each item we take a passage containing a short answer (at most four words, appearing verbatim in the passage), and build two versions: the full passage, and the same passage with the answer string replaced by ____ . The prompt is the passage, then Question: , then the question, then Answer: .A sender reads the full version; the reader gets the masked version. We reserve the final token of the prompt so that decoding begins from a cache of the preceding tokens, generate eight tokens greedily, and score a hit if the normalized answer string appears in the normalized generation. Contexts are capped at 256 tokens and 𝑛 =200 evaluation items are drawn from the validation split. The reader throughout is Qwen2.5-1.5B: 28 layers, grouped-query attention with 12 query heads over 2 key/value heads (group size 6), head dimension 128, hidden width 1536, held frozen. In the augmentation arms the sender is a second instance of the same frozen checkpoint reading a different context, so those results are within-checkpoint and we claim nothing broader; the cross-family arm replaces it with GPT-Neo-1.3B. The mis-sourced negative control holds the reader, the query, and the shared-cache length fixed and draws the sender’s cache from a different probe item, so an arm that scores on it is scoring without using the sender’s content. Both anchors are re-measured inside every run and never carried over. The floor is the reader decoding from its own masked cache. The answer is genuinely absent, so this is the rate at which the question is answerable from the remaining passage, 0 .180 here. The native-cache reference is the reader decoding from its own cache of the full passage, 0 .665. Because the anchors are measured per run, they move with sample size: the same quantities come out at 0 .20 and 0 .742 on a 120-item draw, so we never mix numbers across runs with different anchors, and every accuracy in this paper is quoted against the 0 .180/0 .665 pair.

# H The Sparse-Damage Experiment 

The experiment specified in Section 6 has been run, at the anchors and sample size used throughout ( 𝑛 =200, floor 0 .180, native-cache reference 0 .665), sweeping 𝑝 over {0.01 , 0.05 , 0.10 , 0.25 , 0.50 , 1.0} with three seeds. A fraction 1 − 𝑝 of key/value features is held bit-exact and the remainder replaced with noise at matched per-coordinate power. We report it here rather than in the body because three of its four readings inform the objection without settling it, and because one of them is easy to over-read. 

Sparse damage is not gentler than dense damage at equal fidelity. This is the reading that bears on the objection. Each sparse condition is paired with a dense perturbation calibrated to the same achieved cosine. The two agree throughout: at 𝑝 =0.01 (achieved cosine 0 .9996) sparse scores 0 .197 against dense 0 .177, and at 𝑝 =0.05 (cosine 0 .980) sparse 0 .003 against dense 0 .008. Concentrating the same displacement into a few coordinates neither spares the reader nor harms it more. The premise that attention might route around a small number of badly reproduced features is not supported, and the Section 3.1 result does not need narrowing to dense perturbations specifically. 

What the accuracy-against-𝑝 sweep can and cannot show. Accuracy falls from 0 .180 at 𝑝 =0 to 0 .003 by 𝑝 =0.05, so the fall-away is abrupt rather than smooth, as predicted. But this leg admits no positive finding, and the reason is structural: the stimulus is the reader’s own cache with the answer span masked, so the floor is simultaneously the ceiling and no perturbation can raise the score. The 0 .197 at 𝑝 =0.01 therefore sits at the floor, not above it ( 𝑧 =+0.43 against the floor, with the zero-damage control at 0 .192 bracketing the difference). In particular the pairing of cosine 0 .9996 with 0 .197 is not an instance of the decoupling this paper reports: here high fidelity accompanies preserved function, which is fidelity predicting the outcome correctly. Read as a measurement of self-cache fragility the leg is informative; read as a transfer result it inverts.

# J Fitted Bridges: Scope of the Negative Claim 

Two further features of the fitted-bridge results discussed in Section 1 mark where the remaining problem sits. Both settings are within-family, with matching head geometry and a shared tokenizer, and cross-family transfer is left open. The fitted maps have a limit of their own, retention varying sharply across pairs and tasks, which Heo et al. (2026) attribute to where the residual error lands, not to how large it is, the conclusion Section 3.1 reaches from the other direction. Liu et al. (2026) mark the redundancy boundary from the systems side: reuse across fine-tunes of a shared foundation is genuinely portable, but a fraction of layers must still be recomputed on the receiver, which is a reader-side correction. The more informative difference from our own arms is what gets optimized: Dery et al. (2026) train against a behavioral objective on the receiver instead of reconstruction of its cache, an axis Section 6 identifies as insufficiently varied here. 16
