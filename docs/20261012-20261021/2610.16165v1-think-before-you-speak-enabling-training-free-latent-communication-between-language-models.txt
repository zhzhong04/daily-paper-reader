# Think Before You Speak: Enabling Training-Free Latent Communication Between Language Models 

Rishi Khare 

Georgia Institute of Technology 

## Abstract 

Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabili-ties in solving complex tasks in coding, mathematical logic, and iterative reasoning, especially by coordinating effectively within multi-agent sys-tems (MAS). However, language agents traditionally communicate via text exchange, which introduces a bottleneck in information transfer and adds prefill latency for downstream agents. Recent work proposes that directly sharing the key-value (KV) cache between language models can enable efficient latent collaboration, entirely bypassing standard text-based communication. To enable latent communication between heterogeneous models of different sizes, prior work introduces trained fusion adapters for each model pair, which limits practical deployment. We introduce CacheFuse, a training-free method which demonstrates that augmenting a downstream model with an upstream model’s KV cache using linear in-terpolation consistently enables superior performance than the standalone downstream model can achieve. We systematically analyze CacheFuse across four reasoning and knowledge benchmarks and demonstrate up to 8.6 percentage points higher accuracy compared to single-model baselines and up to 12.2 × TTFT speedup compared to text-based communication in multi-model inference.

## 1 Introduction 

Multi-agent LLM systems have become a dominant paradigm for coordinating multiple spe-cialized models to perform complex tasks, including mathematical reasoning and software development. However, agents conventionally exchange information as natural-language text, so every inter-model exchange requires the sharer model to autoregressively decode a message and the receiver model to prefill the message. This text exchange dominates the time-to-first-token (TTFT) latency of downstream agents, so memory and KV cache man-agement continue to be pervasive challenges in managing the deployment of multi-agent systems (Yu et al., 2026). A promising and practical alternative is latent communication, in which one model transfers its internal KV cache directly to another model rather than communicating through text, allowing the receiver to attend to the sharer’s cached keys and values without recomputation. Recent work demonstrates that this direct transfer can enhance task quality and speed while reducing memory consumption (Zou et al., 2026). However, existing methods for transfer across heterogeneous models, Cache-to-Cache (C2C) and Latent Cache Flow (LCF), train neural adapters that learn to project and fuse one model’s cache into another model’s representation space (Fu et al., 2026; Rossi et al., 2026). Because the fuser is learned uniquely per model pair, deploying the prior methods across a multi-agent communication network of n models requires training up to O(n2) pair-wise adapters, which limits production serving practicality. Motivated by this challenge, we seek to explore practical alternatives to fuse the intelligence of larger upstream models with downstream cheaper-to-serve models in multi-model 1arXiv:2610.16165v1 [cs.CL] 12 Oct 2026 Published at the COLM 2026 Workshop on Efficient Reasoning inference workloads without training learned adapters. We observe that some model families are released across various sizes, and the models’ capability generally scales with increasing model sizes (Kaplan et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2025). Particular pairs of these cross-size models share identical attention geometry, with scaling in the hidden width and number of query heads. Within such an inter-model congruence, two different-sized models share an identical KV-cache shape (the same number of key/value heads, head dimension, and depth), so the sharer’s per-layer cache tensors are dimensionally compatible and can be directly inserted into the receiver model. Based on this observation, we introduce CacheFuse, a training-free method to perform KV cache transfer and fusion to enable cross-model KV cache transfer between models of different size and capability. Our core finding is that linearly interpolating the per-layer KV caches of the sharer and receiver models improves the task performance of the downstream answering model. A linear interpolation of the receiver’s own cache with a transferred upstream cache raises accuracy by up to 8.6 pp with no training. Further, fusion can exceed both the receiver and sharer models individually. Remarkably, we find that a Qwen3-4B downstream model fused with a Qwen3-8B cache even exceeds the standalone 8B model across all of our four benchmarks. We find that downstream models in large-to-small model setups experience a higher lift in accuracy from cache fusion compared to small-to-large setups. Lastly, we demonstrate that direct cache transfer and augmentation is far more efficient than text-based message sharing between models, leading to up to a 12.2 × TTFT reduction over text-to-text communication. 

## 2 Related Work 

2.1 Cross-Model KV Cache Sharing 

The development of KV cache compression and reuse frameworks is an active research area in the pursuit of memory-efficient LLM inference methods. Cache-to-Cache (Fu et al., 2026) introduces a pair-wise trained fuser to enable KV cache augmentation across model architectures bypassing text generation after prefilling both the sharer and receiver mod-els. Latent Cache Flow (Rossi et al., 2026) augments learned cache-level communication by demonstrating that learned cross-model cache sharing adapters can be compressed to consume less system memory. However, these methods involve training pair-wise adapters to enable coherent KV cache fusion between agents, which limits practical deployment in serving systems. DroidSpeak (Liu et al., 2025) enables KV cache sharing by demonstrating that selectively profiling and recomputing critical layers recovers quality. However, their setting focuses on sharing KV caches between downstream finetuned variants of a single base model, rather than heterogeneous transfer across models of different sizes and capa-bility. Unlike prior methods, which involve learned pair-specific adapters for cross-model transfer, CacheFuse only requires that the two models produce KV caches that share the same geometry and combines them with a closed-form linear interpolation. 

2.2 Multi-Agent KV Cache Transfer 

Multi-agent architectures for LLM inference have become a mainstream strategy to pool the intelligence of specialized models through debate and coordination before producing a final answer. Recent work has shown that the time-to-first-token (TTFT) can be significantly reduced by sharing the raw KV cache to future agents, rather than recomputing it from scratch. KVCOMM (Ye et al., 2025) proposes KV cache reuse of previous agent outputs by aligning cache offsets for overlapping content under different prompt prefixes. CacheBlend (Yao et al., 2025) and RelayCaching (Geng et al., 2026) demonstrate that token profiling and selective recomputation can boost the performance of raw KV cache reuse. ## 3 Method 

3.1 Direct KV Cache Transfer 

During autoregressive generation, each decoder layer caches a key and value vector for each processed token for each KV head. For a prompt of length S tokens, the generated KV cache has the shape [nkv , S, dhead ], where nkv is the number of key-value heads and dhead is the per-head dimension. A model with L layers maintains L such key and value tensors, so its KV cache shape can be defined by the dimensions (nkv , dhead , L). In our framing, two models enable a well-defined direct cache transfer when their KV geometries match: identical nkv , dhead , and L ensure that the sharer’s per-layer cache has exactly the shape the receiver expects at each layer, so they can be directly inserted at the same layer position. Our method works by sharing the KV cache of a sharer model to be used directly by a downstream receiver model, with the assumption that the sharer and receiver model share the same KV cache dimensions. The Qwen3 (Yang et al., 2025) family encompasses model pairs with this key property of sharing the same KV cache geometry. Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-4B both have 36 decoder layers and share the KV cache dimensions of (8, 128, 36). Similarly, Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-0.6B have 28 decoder layers and share (8, 128, 28) as their KV cache dimensions. For this reason, we investigate the reuse of KV cache between these pairs, in which the number of decoder layers and KV cache geometry are identical for well-defined transfer, in both directions. 

3.2 Fusion 

Unlike the learned fusion gates of C2C, we investigated the blending of the caches between the sharer and receiver models through a per-layer linear interpolation between KV caches as follows (shown in Equation (1) and Figure 1): 

(K, V)fused = θ (K, V)sharer + ( 1 − θ) ( K, V)receiver (1) In this setting, the single scalar θ ∈ [0, 1 ] controls the proportion of the key and value tensors uniformly across layers from the sharer model that will be interpolated into the cache of the receiver model. At the two endpoints: • θ = 0: use the receiver’s own cache without any transfer • θ = 1: entire replacement with the sharer’s cache 

## 4 Experimental Setup 

We set up multi-model benchmarks analogous to the evaluation of Cache-to-Cache and Latent Cache Flow, in which a sharer model’s KV cache is directly transferred to a receiver model to decode, without any text exchange. We analyze latent cache sharing across pairs of varying sizes (0.6B, 1.7B, 4B, 8B) of the Qwen3 (Yang et al., 2025) model architecture. 

4.1 Approaches 

For each benchmark, we evaluate five approaches. 3Published at the COLM 2026 Workshop on Efficient Reasoning • Receiver : the receiver model decodes normally from its own prefill ( θ = 0, single-model baseline, no inter-model communication) • Sharer : the sharer model decodes its own prefill (single-model baseline, no inter-model communication) • Replace : the receiver model decodes from the transferred upstream cache entirely (θ = 1) • CacheFuse : the receiver model decodes using an interpolated cache across θ ∈ (0, 1 )

• Text-to-Text (T2T) : text communication baseline in which the sharer model gen-erates a one-sentence hint that is prepended to the receiver’s prompt Crucially, the evaluation results we report are conducted so that for each question, the receiver’s cache and the sharer’s (transferred) cache are each prefilled exactly once. In order to maintain fair and consistent evaluation, each sweep on the sharer model is executed by performing linear interpolation on the same prefill caches for the sharer and receiver models and only varying the single θ parameter across the [0, 1 ] range. This makes the evaluation setups controlled, as all approaches per model pair (Receiver, Sharer, Replace, CacheFuse) are scored using the exact same prefill caches to mitigate prefill variation. However, T2T is an exception to this property as it must run decode on its hint before the receiver can prefill its prompt. See Appendix A for more description of the exact prompt templates we use. 

4.2 Benchmarks 

Mirroring the Latent Cache Flow evaluation suite, we analyze the accuracy of CacheFuse on four multiple-choice benchmarks: MMLU-Redux (Gema et al., 2025), MMLU-Pro (Wang et al., 2024), ARC-Challenge (Clark et al., 2018), and OpenBookQA (Mihaylov et al., 2018). All setups use the same generation budgets as C2C, so the text baseline is measured under the same budget the learned gating method reports. In the T2T setting, the sharer model is prompted to provide a hint which is decoded up to 256 tokens (same as C2C’s T2T maximum generation limit). For the latency measurement, the answer is decoded up to 64 tokens (same as C2C’s maximum response length for multiple choice). Similarly to LCF, each response is scored as an argmax of the next-token logits restricted to the option-letter tokens, so the accuracy is independent of generation limits and decode settings. 

4.3 Latency 

We adopt the same latency measurements as Latent Cache Flow: mean per-query time-to-first-token (TTFT) and time-to-end-of-answer (TTEoA). TTFT is computed after the first decoded token, so it includes all prefill, transfer, and fusion steps. All of our latency measurements are reported on a single NVIDIA A5000 GPU (24GB VRAM).

5.3 Replacement Compatibility 

Direct cache replacement ( θ = 1) serves as an informative signal on raw cache compatibility. In every pair apart from 8B → 4B, direct cache replacement approximately collapses to the regime of random chance correctness in multiple-choice question-answering, which indicates that a raw cross-model cache is generally not directly usable. However, for the 8B 

→ 4B pair, the raw 8B cache is highly usable on its own (the replacement strategy achieves about 83.5% on ARC-C), though the reverse direction (4B → 8B) experiences much lower coherence (39.2% on ARC-C).

## 6 Conclusion 

CacheFuse demonstrates that answering from another model’s latent representation instead of text can be accomplished across models of different sizes without a trained adapter, given the setting involves sharing the cache between models with congruent KV cache geometries. Our findings demonstrate that across both large-to-small model and small-to-large model KV cache transfers, linear interpolation between the models’ KV caches (i) improves the performance of the downstream model, (ii) can exceed both models in the pair, and (iii) substantially reduces TTFT over text-based communication. 

## Limitations 

In order to perform direct KV cache transfer, CacheFuse requires the sharer and receiver caches to share the same KV cache dimensions for well-defined reuse. In addition, our experimental setup analyzes model pairs that share the same tokenizer (Qwen3 in this 7Published at the COLM 2026 Workshop on Efficient Reasoning work). An exciting direction for future work is the development of methods for cross-model KV cache transfer across entirely heterogeneous tokenizer and model architecture families (i.e. Qwen to Llama).
