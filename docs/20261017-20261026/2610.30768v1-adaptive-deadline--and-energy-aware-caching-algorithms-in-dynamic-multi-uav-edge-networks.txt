A B S T R A C T 

Content caching via unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) becomes one of the solution to maintain content delivery when terrestrial infrastructure is unavailable or disrupted. However, existing caching approaches often focus primarily on cache-hit performance or average delivery delay, without jointly addressing spatially heterogeneous and time-varying demand, content-specific deadlines, and uneven communication-energy consumption across UAVs. To address these challenges, we formulate a dynamic deadline- and energy-aware cache placement and replacement problem that jointly considers deadline misses, UAV-side communication energy, and inter-UAV energy imbalance. To achieve this, we first develop deadline- and energy-aware UBAC (DE-UBAC), an interpretable hierarchical rule-based realization that incorporates deadline-aware prioritization, cooperative placement, energy-aware coordination, and cache-update cost. However, predefined priorities and thresholds limit its adaptability under dynamically coupled network conditions. We therefore propose GRU-assisted Deadline- and Energy-aware Proximal Policy Optimization (GRU-DEPPO), which combines local and global GRU-based temporal representations with a centralized PPO actor–critic for adaptive cache placement and replacement. Simulation results show that GRU-DEPPO achieves the lowest deadline-miss rate and total UAV-side communication energy among the considered schemes. Compared with DE-UBAC, GRU-DEPPO reduces the deadline-miss rate by approximately 61% and inter-UAV energy imbalance by approximately 40%. 

## 1. Introduction 

Recent advances in the computational and sensing capa-bilities of end devices, coupled with the rapid proliferation of mobile and Internet of Things (IoT) devices, have led to a substantial increase in the volume of data generated at the network edge [1, 2]. At the same time, emerging applications have imposed increasingly diverse and strin-gent requirements in terms of latency, reliability, and band-width. Conventional cloud computing, which provides large-scale computing resources through centralized data centers, faces several limitations in satisfying these requirements: (i) communication latency caused by geographical distance and the traversal of multiple network segments, (ii) latency variations and jitter resulting from network congestion, (iii) increasing backhaul traffic, and (iv) degraded service avail-ability under disruptions in wide-area network connectivity. To address these limitations, edge computing has attracted considerable attention as a distributed computing paradigm that places computing and storage resources closer to end devices and data sources [3, 4]. By processing data at or near the network edge, edge computing can reduce service la-tency and alleviate the traffic burden on backhaul networks. In particular, unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV)-assisted edge computing has attracted significant attention because it enables communication, storage, and computing resources

to be rapidly and flexibly deployed in environments where fixed base stations and edge infrastructure are unavailable, insufficient, or unable to respond promptly to abrupt changes in service demand [5–7]. Owing to their controllable mobil-ity and adjustable altitude, UAVs can be rapidly deployed and repositioned to increase the probability of establishing line-of-sight (LoS) links with ground devices and dynam-ically adjust communication coverage. They can also ac-commodate temporary surges in traffic demand and provide temporary network services in areas where terrestrial com-munication infrastructure is sparse, unavailable, or damaged. These characteristics make UAV-assisted edge computing particularly useful in remote environments, such as moun-tainous and maritime regions; temporarily crowded venues, such as concerts and sporting events; and disaster-affected areas where terrestrial base stations or wired backhaul links have been damaged by earthquakes or wildfires [8]. Despite these advantages, UAV-assisted networks face several inherent limitations, including energy constraints imposed by limited battery capacity, restricted onboard com-puting and storage resources, and limited backhaul capacity between UAVs and terrestrial networks [9]. In particular, forwarding user requests to a remote content server or central cloud through a macro base station (MBS) may increase backhaul traffic and content delivery latency. The associated backhaul transmissions may also consume substantial com-munication energy, thereby accelerating battery depletion and shortening the operational lifetime of UAVs. A promis-ing approach to reducing backhaul dependence, content de-livery latency, and communication energy consumption is to equip UAVs with storage and employ them as aerial cache nodes that proactively store selected content [10]. When the 1arXiv:2610.30768v1 [cs.LG] 22 Oct 2026 requested content is available in the local cache of the serv-ing UAV, the UAV can deliver it directly to the user without accessing the MBS or retrieving the content from a remote server. If the content is not cached locally but is available at a neighboring UAV, it can instead be obtained through cooperative content sharing, further reducing reliance on the terrestrial backhaul. Such local and cooperative cache hits can reduce content delivery latency, backhaul utilization, communication energy consumption, and overall network resource usage. However, because UAVs have limited storage capac-ity, the entire content catalog cannot be cached. Moreover, content-demand and user-mobility patterns may vary across different regions and over time, while different content types may have heterogeneous delivery deadlines. A cache miss may therefore require content retrieval through the terrestrial backhaul, incurring additional delay and communication energy consumption and potentially causing the request to exceed its deadline [11, 12]. Cache replacement also entails a non-negligible cost because fetching and storing new content requires additional data transmission and communication energy. Furthermore, uneven service loads across UAVs may result in imbalanced communication-energy consumption. Consequently, maximizing the cache hit ratio or selecting content solely according to long-term popularity may be insufficient in dynamic multi-UAV environments [13–15]. An effective caching strategy should therefore adapt cache placement to changes in content demand and network con-ditions while jointly reducing the deadline-miss rate, the communication energy consumed by request delivery and cache updates, and inter-UAV energy imbalance [16]. These improvements can also reduce dependence on MBS-assisted content retrieval. To address these challenges, we first develop an inter-pretable deadline- and energy-aware rule-based caching de-sign and then propose GRU-assisted Deadline- and Energy-aware PPO (GRU-DEPPO) to overcome the limitations of predefined cache-decision rules under dynamic multi-UAV conditions. GRU-DEPPO periodically determines cache placement and replacement decisions by jointly considering content-specific delivery deadlines, the communication en-ergy incurred by request delivery and cache updates, and the distribution of communication energy consumption across UAVs. By using local and global GRUs to capture temporal patterns from UAV-local and network-wide observations, the proposed scheme adapts to spatially heterogeneous and time-varying network conditions while reducing deadline misses, communication energy consumption, and inter-UAV energy imbalance. The main contributions of this study are as follows: • Dynamic deadline- and energy-aware multi-UAV caching formulation: We formulate a dynamic cache placement and replacement problem for multi-UAV edge networks with spatially heterogeneous and time-varying content demand and content-specific deliv-ery deadlines. The formulation jointly considers the deadline-miss rate, UAV-side communication energy incurred by request delivery and cache updates, and inter-UAV communication-energy imbalance, subject to cache-capacity and cache-update feasibility con-straints. • Deadline- and energy-aware cache decision design: 

We develop DE-UBAC as an interpretable hierarchi-cal realization of the formulated caching problem. It extends user-behavior-aware caching with deadline-miss-aware prioritization, persistent-miss feedback, deadline-feasible cooperative placement, energy-aware cache coordination, and cache-update-cost-aware re-placement. By assigning these factors distinct roles in the cache-decision process, DE-UBAC provides a structured rule-based realization of the proposed problem and reveals the limitations of predefined decision rules under dynamic multi-UAV conditions. • GRU-assisted adaptive caching policy: To over-come the limitations of the above rule-based design, we develop GRU-DEPPO as the learning-based so-lution to adaptively optimize cache placement and replacement under dynamically coupled network con-ditions. A shared local GRU captures UAV-specific temporal evolution, while a global GRU encodes network-wide temporal context at the MBS. Their representations are fused within a centralized PPO actor–critic that jointly learns feasible cache updates from deadline, demand, connectivity, and energy con-ditions, avoiding the fixed priorities and thresholds required by the rule-based design. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Sec-tion 2 reviews related work on UAV-assisted edge networks and edge caching. Section 3 presents the system model and formulates the deadline- and energy-aware cache place-ment and replacement problem. Section 4 first develops a deadline- and energy-aware rule-based extension of UBAC and discusses its limitations under dynamic network condi-tions, and then presents the proposed GRU-DEPPO scheme for learning adaptive cache placement and replacement de-cisions. Section 5 evaluates the proposed approaches under various experimental settings and compares them with base-line schemes. Finally, Section 6 concludes the paper and discusses directions for future research.

Game-theoretic approaches have also been used to co-ordinate caching and resource allocation in UAV-assisted networks. The study in [14] modeled cooperative content transmission among UAVs using a coalition formation game, while [20] employed a Stackelberg–Bertrand game to jointly consider content caching and service economics. The work in [21] considered content caching, service placement, and task offloading using Gibbs sampling and a matching game. These approaches provide structured mechanisms for coor-dinating multiple network entities, but they generally rely on predefined utility or preference models and do not learn tem-poral demand dynamics from historical observations. More-over, they do not jointly address cache-update energy, inter-UAV energy imbalance, and request-level deadline misses under content-specific deadlines. Learning-based approaches have been investigated to improve adaptability in dynamic UAV networks. The work in [22] combined differential evolution for content caching with multi-agent PPO for UAV trajectory optimization. In [23], a dueling DQN jointly determined UAV positions and cache placement using user-location and request infor-mation, whereas [24] employed dual-clip PPO to jointly optimize caching, trajectory, and transmission power in a single-UAV setting. More recently, the work in [25] used an Informer model to predict user request and mobility pat-terns and incorporated these predictions into a PPO-based deployment framework. ## 3. System Model and Problem Formulation 

In the considered scenario illustrated in Fig. 1, the local base station is unavailable due to a failure, and a remote MBS stores the global content pool and provides backhaul access to cache-enabled UAVs. Users are divided into groups, with each group characterized by group-specific mobility patterns and content-request distributions that are shared by its users. Each user associates with the nearest UAV having a feasible access link. Requests may be served by the associated UAV, 3a neighboring UAV, or the MBS, depending on content avail-ability and link feasibility [26]. The selected path determines the request delay and communication energy consumption. Users move within the service area and generate requests according to group-level preferences and request-generation probabilities. Each generated request follows a strict source-selection priority. For a request associated with UAV 𝑛 , the local cache is checked first. If the requested item is not available locally, UAV 𝑛 searches for feasible cooperative paths to UAVs caching the item and selects the path with the smallest end-to-end transmission delay. Only when no feasible cooperative cache path exists is the item retrieved from the MBS through the feasible direct or relayed backhaul path with the smallest end-to-end transmission delay. If no feasible delivery path is available owing to link outages or insufficient achievable rates, the corresponding request is dropped and excluded from the delivered-request set. Cache placement is updated at predefined intervals based on the observed network state. Newly cached content is transferred either directly from the MBS to the target UAV or through one or more UAV relay hops. To model nonsta-tionarity, a change point is randomly selected from the latter half of each episode, at which both the mobility context and local request distributions shift. Problem (19) is a stochastic combinatorial optimization problem. Even when user locations, requests, and link condi-tions are fixed, the formulation contains a static cooperative cache-placement subproblem with binary placement deci-sions and per-UAV cache-capacity constraints. Cooperative cache-placement problems of this form have been shown to be NP-hard through a reduction from the 2-Disjoint Set Cov-ers problem [35]. Therefore, Problem (19), which further incorporates time-varying demand, user mobility, content-specific deadlines, communication energy consumption, and inter-UAV energy imbalance, is also NP-hard. The number of feasible cache placements grows com-binatorially with the network size, making exact offline optimization computationally prohibitive for large-scale net-works. Moreover, repeatedly solving the problem at each cache-decision interval is impractical under dynamically changing requests, mobility, and link conditions. We there-fore first investigate a structured rule-based realization of the formulated problem and then develop a learning-based policy to overcome the limitations of predefined cache-decision rules. 

## 4. Adaptive Deadline- and Energy-Aware Cache Algorithms 

In this section, we first investigate how the deadline-and energy-aware cache placement and replacement prob-lem formulated in Section 3 can be addressed through a rule-based extension of the local-demand-aware UBAC scheme [19]. To capture the temporal evolution of spatially heterogeneous local and network-wide conditions, GRU-DEPPO employs local and global GRU-based representations, which are subsequently provided to a centralized PPO actor–critic for joint cache-update decision making. 4.1. Deadline- and Energy-Aware Rule-Based Caching 

Caching decisions based primarily on content popularity or local demand can improve content availability around cur-rent request locations. However, such decisions may be in-sufficient when contents have heterogeneous delivery dead-lines and UAVs experience uneven communication-energy burdens. In particular, the preference for local cache service does not imply that every highly requested content should be replicated at its associated UAV. A content with a rela-tively relaxed deadline may still be served through a nearby cooperative cache within its deadline, allowing limited local cache space to be reserved for more deadline-sensitive con-tents. Moreover, repeatedly concentrating cache and service loads on particular UAVs may increase inter-UAV energy imbalance. To incorporate these considerations into an interpretable rule-based strategy, we extend UBAC [19] into a deadline-and energy-aware variant, referred to as DE-UBAC. DE-UBAC retains the user-behavior-aware counting and coor-dinated cache-management structure of UBAC, including handover-aware history updates and MBS-level cache co-ordination, while introducing deadline-, cooperation-, and energy-aware decision rules. 5.1. Simulation setup 

We consider a multi-UAV service area in which users are divided into multiple groups with distinct mobility and content-demand characteristics. 

5.1.1. User mobility and spatial locality 

To model heterogeneous local conditions, users follow a group hotspot-aware mobility model. Users are divided into several behavior groups, and each group is associated with a spatial hotspot representing its preferred area of activity. Users within the same group share the same hotspot region, while their movement trajectories are generated indepen-dently. At the same change point, the group-conditioned content-demand distributions are also shifted to represent temporal changes in user preferences.

In contrast, GRU-DEPPO explicitly incorporates inter-UAV energy imbalance into its decision objective, discouraging cache and service decisions that repeatedly concentrate communication load on a sub-set of UAVs. In particular, among the MBS-direct UAVs that also serve as relay gateways for edge UAVs without direct MBS connectivity, GRU-DEPPO achieves an energy-consumption CV of 0.568, compared with 0.977 for LRU, 0.908 for DE-UBAC, and 0.922 for TLPPO. Overall, GRU-DEPPO effectively captures the temporal evolution of spatially heterogeneous local network condi-tions through its GRU-based representations. Moreover, by explicitly incorporating content-specific deadlines and inter-UAV energy imbalance into the PPO-based decision frame-work, rather than focusing solely on average delivery delay or cache-hit performance, GRU-DEPPO achieves a better overall trade-off among deadline satisfaction, communica-tion energy consumption, and inter-UAV energy imbalance. Consequently, tighter deadline requirements can expose environment-intrinsic performance limitations that are difficult to fully mitigate through cache placement alone. Although GRU-DEPPO exhibits somewhat higher DMRs for contents with the relatively relaxed 3.0-s and 4.0-s deadlines, it achieves a substantial reduction in DMR for contents with the most stringent 2.0-s dead-line. This result suggests that, as the delivery requirements become more stringent, the advantage of GRU-DEPPO becomes increasingly concentrated on the most deadline-sensitive contents. In other words, the policy places greater emphasis on satisfying requests with tighter deadline re-quirements rather than uniformly reducing the DMR of all content types. Despite the slightly higher DMRs for the relatively re-laxed content types, GRU-DEPPO achieves a 24.0% lower overall DMR than TLPPO under the stringent deadline set-ting. This result indicates that, in the observed performance trade-off, the substantial reduction in deadline misses for the most deadline-sensitive content outweighs the smaller performance degradations for the relatively relaxed content types. Moreover, as shown in Figs. 4c and 4d, GRU-DEPPO maintains its advantages in both total communication energy consumption and inter-UAV energy imbalance. These re-sults indicate that GRU-DEPPO maintains a favorable over-all performance trade-off even when the content-delivery requirements become substantially more stringent. LRU and DE-UBAC incur relatively low cache-update energy among the compared schemes, but for different reasons. In contrast, DE-UBAC explicitly suppresses a replacement when the ex-pected priority gain is insufficient to justify the correspond-ing update-energy cost, thereby avoiding low-benefit cache updates. ## 6. Conclusion 

In this paper, we investigated deadline- and energy-aware cache placement and replacement in dynamic multi-UAV edge networks with spatially heterogeneous and time-varying content demand. We first developed DE-UBAC as an interpretable hierarchical rule-based realization of the formulated caching objectives and used its limitations under dynamically coupled network conditions to motivate the proposed GRU-DEPPO framework. GRU-DEPPO employs local and global GRU-based temporal representations to capture the evolution of UAV-local and network-wide con-ditions and provides the resulting representations to a cen-tralized PPO policy. Simulation results showed that GRU-DEPPO achieves the lowest deadline-miss rate and total communication energy consumption among the considered schemes while substantially reducing inter-UAV energy im-balance. As future work, we plan to extend the proposed frame-work by jointly optimizing UAV positioning and content caching.
